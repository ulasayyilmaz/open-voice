Structures

The model is a simplified, schematic midline section, built to show relationships (what sits where and what narrows what). It is not a medical-grade scan.

A throat-dominant voice is rarely caused by one solid blockage. Usually several muscular habits narrow the tube at the same time, and all of them can be trained:

  1. The head pokes forward, so the larynx is pulled up.
  2. The tongue root is pulled back into the throat.
  3. The throat walls (constrictors) squeeze.
  4. The false vocal folds squeeze.
  5. The soft palate is passive.
About the mouth-to-nose path: an "open" singing tone is mostly not air going through the nose. On sung vowels the soft palate is mostly raised, though some trained singers keep a small opening. The open quality comes from a long, wide throat and freely vibrating folds. The "mask" or nose sensation is vibration, and it gets stronger once the throat stops squeezing.

Exercises change how the muscles shape the tube: larynx height, tongue position, wall tension, fold behavior. They don't remove tissue. Change builds over weeks and months of regular practice.
